# Worked examples and claim rules

## Approved Apple TV example

This is a modelled UK worked example, not a typical-user result and not a guaranteed saving.

### Inputs

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| Episodes | 10 |
| Release cadence | One episode at premiere, then weekly |
| Premiere | 3 April 2026 |
| Finale | 5 June 2026 |
| Release span | 63 days |
| Assumed subscription start | 3 April 2026 |
| Assumed monthly renewal day | The 3rd |
| UK monthly price | £9.99 |
| Alternative watch start | After the 5 June finale |
| Alternative viewing period | One paid month |
| Other wanted Apple TV viewing in the release window | None |

### Exact arithmetic

There are nine weekly intervals between the first and tenth episode:

```text
release span = 7 × (10 - 1)
             = 63 days
```

Starting at the premiere crosses three assumed billing dates:

```text
3 April 2026  = £9.99
3 May 2026    = £9.99
3 June 2026   = £9.99
premiere strategy total = 3 × £9.99 = £29.97
```

Waiting until the finale and watching in one month requires one charge:

```text
wait strategy total = 1 × £9.99 = £9.99
```

The illustrative difference is:

```text
potential saving = £29.97 - £9.99
                 = £19.98
                 = two £9.99 renewal charges
```

Do not calculate the release window as `10 / 4` months. A ten-episode weekly season has nine weekly gaps, and calendar subscriptions renew on actual billing dates rather than four-week blocks.

## Exact qualification

Use this wording without shortening it when the £19.98 result appears:

> Example assumes a UK £9.99 monthly plan, subscription starting 3 April 2026, monthly renewal on the 3rd, watching after the 5 June finale, and no other Apple TV viewing during the release window. Prices and dates can change.

Use this source line:

> Sources: Apple release schedule and Apple TV UK price. Checked 23 August 2026.

Short asset labels such as “Assumptions + sources in caption” are only acceptable when the exact qualification and source line are in the same post and the result is clearly labelled as a worked example.

## Approved wording

- “Stop paying for the wait.”
- “10 weekly episodes. 3 billing dates. 1 month to binge.”
- “Potential saving: £19.98.”
- “Waiting until the finale could avoid two £9.99 renewals in this worked example.”
- “Seasons helps you time subscriptions around the shows you want.”
- “Weekly releases can keep a streaming subscription running for months.”
- “Build my savings plan.”

## Forbidden wording

- “Users save £19.98.”
- “Seasons saves you £19.98.”
- “Save £19.98 guaranteed.”
- “Users save £X per year.”
- Any annual number produced by multiplying this one-show example.
- “Up to £X” without representative evidence and advertising review.
- “Seasons cancels your subscription.”
- “Seasons guarantees the cheapest way to stream.”
- Any claim that three billing dates are an average for ten-episode seasons.
- Any claim that a cancellation action is the same as a matured, verified avoided charge.

## Eligibility rules for future examples

Publish a currency result only when the service, region, exact monthly tier, price, currency, schedule, and billing dates are known and dated. The user must be able to end the plan without a remaining minimum term. The title must remain available through the proposed watch window. Other wanted content on the same billable product must be considered, and overlapping title windows must not count the same avoided charge twice.

Annual plans, bundles, trials, discounts, student offers, carrier packages, minimum-term plans, and subscriptions with non-video benefits need separate treatment. When price or eligibility cannot be established, publish the timing mechanism without a currency figure.
